With time free to take any shape, the giants show up and concentration fades

Letting each month have its own coefficient — instead of forcing the path to track the exchange rate — reverses the earlier reading. Categories holding one of the five giants ran 2 to 4% more expensive through 2024–2025 (joint p = 0.0003), then converged to zero during 2026. The CR3 term, once giants are in the model, no longer clears significance (p = 0.14).

Revises the exchange-rate specification

The earlier version interacted both terms with log(USD/ILS). That imposes a single shape: the differential must move in proportion to the currency, which trends almost monotonically. CR3's gap does trend that way, so it loaded onto the exchange rate and looked significant. The giant gap is a hump — it opens in 2024, holds through 2025, closes in 2026 — which is close to orthogonal to a monotone trend, so that specification could not see it. Freeing the time path is what surfaces it.

The lagged exchange rate, for completeness

If the interaction is put back on the currency at various lags, the picture does not change: CR3 keeps a modest negative term, the giant term stays indistinguishable from zero. A three-month lag is no different from none.

This is why the time specification is the better one here. The lag table asks the same badly-posed question five ways and gets the same answer each time: it can only detect differentials shaped like the currency path. Nothing in the theory says a pass-through gap must be monotone — it can open and close, which is exactly what the giant gap does.

Specification

log Pct = αc + δt + Σt βt·( CR3z,c × 1[month=t] ) + Σt φt·( Giantc × 1[month=t] ) + εct

No exchange rate enters the model at all. 284 categories × 31 months = 8,804 observations, 132 categories with a giant at ≥5% quantity share and 152 without, January 2024 as reference, standard errors clustered by category. The joint tests are F-tests on all 30 month interactions of a term at once, which is the right test for "does this matter over the panel" — reading 30 individual coefficients invites finding one by chance.

The reversal is not an artefact of the giant definition. Joint p for the giant terms is 0.0003 at a ≥5% share, 0.0008 for present-at-all, and 0.0001 when a giant must be the top supplier. CR3's joint p is 0.14, 0.037 and 0.09 across the same three — never robustly significant once giants are included.

What it does not settle. The two terms are correlated: giant categories are more concentrated on average, so separating them leans on the within-tier variation documented earlier (CR3 is near-identical across giant and non-giant categories inside each tier, which is what makes the split legitimate). A gap that opens and closes is also the signature you would expect from a mix shift — promotions, pack sizes — not only from pricing conduct, and a unit-value price cannot tell those apart. Timing is suggestive, not identification: nothing here pins the 2025 gap to a specific cause.
